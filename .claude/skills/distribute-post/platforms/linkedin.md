# Step 4: LinkedIn

A short post in Mihai's own voice: one paragraph on what the article argues and what's in it, a blank line, then the tracked link. Mihai approved this format on 2026-10-17, for the LinkedIn post about "AI Can Write the Code. Who Verifies It?". A punchier draft with a question hook, short stat lines and a closing question was turned down as "tacky".

UTM tags: `utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social`.

## Mihai's LinkedIn style (decided; change it only if Mihai asks)

- One short paragraph, a blank line, then the link. Add a second short paragraph only when it clearly earns its place.
- Plain, earnest and first person. Longer, flowing sentences and small asides in parentheses are fine.
- No hook formulas, short stat lines, emoji, hype, generic closing questions, hashtags or company tags.
- The link goes in the post itself, with UTM tags, not in the first comment.
- Every claim matches the article exactly. Credit the source of any number ("Faros has linked…"), and keep a correlation a correlation.
- If the article offers something (a harness, a checklist, a template), repeat the offer. It gives the right readers a real reason to reply or send a message, which fits the blog's goal: conversations and leads over pageviews.

## What the research says, and how it fits this style

Researched on 2026-11-25. Most numbers come from vendors that sell LinkedIn tools, so treat them as rough. Refresh this section about every six months.

- **The first lines** before "…more" decide whether people keep reading. Make the opening sentence say specifically what the article is about. That works in Mihai's voice without a hook formula.
- **Dwell time** is the most consistently reported ranking signal. A paragraph that states the argument and names what's inside gives readers something to read.
- **Links:** the evidence is mixed. Many guides claim 50–70% less reach for posts with links. The one study with a stated sample (62,820 posts, by postiv.ai) found about 7% less engagement. The first-comment workaround is reported to be curtailed. The link stays in the post.
- **Engagement bait** ("Agree?", "Comment YES") is flagged by LinkedIn's spam rules, and substantive replies count for more than "Thanks!".
- **Editing in the first few minutes** is widely reported to hurt a post. Leave 12 to 18 hours between posts.
- **Not used by default:** native images and document carousels (PDFs of 8 to 10 slides) are reported to get more dwell time and saves. Mihai's approved post used the link preview card. Suggest one only as an experiment Mihai opts into.

## Checks to show Mihai before posting

1. Check the link preview card in the composer, or in [LinkedIn Post Inspector](https://www.linkedin.com/post-inspector/). It should show the cover, title and description.
2. Post at a time when you can reply to comments thoughtfully.
3. Don't edit the post in its first hour unless something is wrong.
4. Reply to comments with substance, and follow up on any requests for what the post offered.
